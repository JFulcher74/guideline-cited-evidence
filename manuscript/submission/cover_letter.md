**Submission: "Guideline citation and the growth of PubMed: a pre-registered cohort study, 2008–2024"**

I submit the above manuscript for consideration as an original research article in *Scientometrics*.

Citations from clinical practice guidelines are widely used as an indicator of the practice relevance of biomedical research, but no study has tested whether the chance that a newly published paper reaches a guideline has changed as the literature has grown. In a pre-registered cohort study, I linked 18.0 million PubMed research articles (2008–2024), through the NIH Open Citation Collection, to the reference lists of 23,920 PubMed-indexed guidelines and 7,943 guidelines from 31 named national and international bodies.

The main findings are:

- Research articles per year grew 4.7% a year to 2020, while papers cited by a guideline within five years grew only 0.6–2.7% a year. The five-year probability of guideline citation fell from 1 in 25 papers to 1 in 43 for PubMed-indexed guidelines, and from 1 in 87 to 1 in 112 for named bodies.
- Guideline citation capacity grew only 0.7–2.7% a year, so the constraint appears to lie on the guideline side.
- In a post hoc analysis restricted to MEDLINE-indexed articles, and within journals already cited by guidelines, the cohort decline was inconclusive. The dilution is concentrated in PubMed's growth outside MEDLINE and outside established journals.

I believe the work suits *Scientometrics* because its central lesson is methodological: indicators built on PubMed totals inherit the database's widening scope, and studies of clinical or policy citation impact should report results for a selectively indexed or stable core of journals alongside the full database. The study adds to the journal's literature on literature growth, database coverage and indicators of societal impact.

The protocol was registered on the Open Science Framework before outcome data were analysed (doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/VN9BQ), and all deviations are reported. Reporting follows a checklist adapted from RECORD (Online Resource 2). All code, extraction snapshots, validation-gate data and processed outputs are public on GitHub and archived on Zenodo.
